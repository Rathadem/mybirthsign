Why Many Khmer Weddings Happen After Buddhist Lent

When the monks' rains retreat ends at Chenh Vassa, many Cambodian families start planning weddings again. Here is where that custom comes from, and why it belongs to families rather than to Buddhist law.

Published October 19, 2026 · MyBirthSign

Who decidesFamilies, with the achar

A season that closes, then reopens

Every year Cambodian monks spend about three lunar months in their own monastery for Vassa, the rains retreat, which Cambodians mark at its start as Chol Vassa and at its end as Chenh Vassa. In 2026 the retreat began on Thursday 30 July and ends on Monday 26 October, the full moon of the month of Assoch. These dates come from the Khmer lunar calendar that powers our Khmer Calendar.

For many lay families, those three months are also a pause in one particular part of life: weddings. Reporting on the start of the 2026 retreat, the French-language news site Lepetitjournal Cambodge noted simply that, by tradition, weddings are not celebrated during the Vassa months. Once the retreat is over, families who have been waiting can set a date.

A custom, not a Buddhist rule

It is easy to assume that Buddhism forbids weddings during Vassa, but the rains retreat is a rule for monks. Thanissaro Bhikkhu's Buddhist Monastic Code, a standard English guide to the Theravada monastic discipline, describes the retreat as an obligation of the bhikkhus (fully ordained monks): they agree to stay in one place for the rainy season, beginning the day after the full moon of Asalhi (Asadh in Khmer), or the second Asadh in a year with two.

The same chapter even assumes that lay weddings can take place during the retreat. Among the reasons a monk may leave his monastery for up to seven days, if he is sent for, it lists a lay follower whose son or daughter is getting married. Nothing in the monks' code tells lay people when they may marry.

So the pause in weddings is a family and community custom. How strictly it is kept differs from family to family and from place to place. Some couples avoid the whole of Vassa; others simply follow the date their elders and the achar recommend.

Practical reasons: rain, fields and guests

Practical life points the same way. The Khmer wedding page on the Tourism of Cambodia website explains that some families do not allow their children to marry in the rainy season, for three plain reasons: heavy rain makes the reception, the procession and other ceremonies difficult; guests find it hard to travel to the wedding; and it is the time when farmers are busy in their fields.

Weather also shapes the look of a Cambodian wedding. A 2016 report in ASEAN News Today described receptions held in marquees set up on sidewalks and roads, and said that the end of the wet season, with its cooler evenings, ushers in the wedding and engagement season. Vassa covers much of the wet season, so a family that waits until the retreat ends is also waiting for drier and more comfortable weather.

How families choose the day

Ending Vassa opens the calendar, but it does not fix the date. According to the Library of Congress country study of Cambodia, families traditionally consult an achar, a lay ceremonial leader, to set the wedding date, and the achar also directs the ceremony itself, which begins in the morning at the bride's home.

The Tourism of Cambodia page records further guidance from researcher Nhean Phoeun. It gives the general principles below, which are traditional guidance rather than fixed law:

  • Khmer tradition allows weddings in only about six months of the year.
  • Weddings are held in months of 30 days. Khmer lunar months normally alternate between 29 and 30 days, as our guide to Khmer leap months explains.
  • Only seven days in each suitable month are considered good days.
  • A wedding should not fall on the couple's birthday, a religious day, a lunar or solar eclipse, or Khmer New Year.
  • Engagement ceremonies, by contrast, can be held in any month.

The same page notes that, because of fortune telling, some families wait as long as two years after an engagement before the wedding.

What this means for 2026 and 2027

With Chenh Vassa on 26 October 2026, families who keep the custom can plan weddings from late October onward. The weeks just after the retreat are busy in other ways too: the Kathen robe-offering season runs from 27 October to 24 November 2026, and the Water Festival public holiday falls on 23–25 November 2026.

Calculated with the same Khmer calendar rules, the next rains retreat is expected to run from 19 July to 15 October 2027. For a family that avoids Vassa, that leaves roughly late October 2026 to mid-July 2027 as the main window. Khmer New Year, which the guidance above says to avoid, is expected on 14–16 April 2027; the official 2027 holiday list has not yet been published, so treat that date as to be confirmed. Any specific day should still be checked with your pagoda or achar, since a local calendar can occasionally differ by a day.

Frequently asked questions

Can Cambodians get married during Buddhist Lent?

Yes. The rains retreat is a rule for monks, and the monastic code even mentions lay weddings during the retreat. Many families still prefer to wait until Vassa ends, as a matter of custom.

When does Buddhist Lent end in 2026?

Vassa ends at Chenh Vassa on Monday 26 October 2026, the full moon of Assoch. The next retreat is expected to run from 19 July to 15 October 2027.

Who chooses the wedding date in a traditional Khmer wedding?

Traditionally the families consult an achar, a lay ceremonial leader, who sets the date and also directs the ceremony. Traditional guidance also says to avoid the couple's birthdays, religious days, eclipses and Khmer New Year.

Is the custom the same in every family?

No. Some families avoid the whole rainy season, some only the three months of Vassa, and some follow other advice entirely. Ask your elders or the achar who will lead the ceremony.
