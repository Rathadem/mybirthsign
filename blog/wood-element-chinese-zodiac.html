The Wood Element: Meaning, Years and Personality in the Chinese Zodiac

Wood is one of the five elements of Chinese tradition, alongside Fire, Earth, Metal and Water. Here is what Wood stands for, which birth years belong to it, and how it works with the other four elements.

Published October 25, 2026 · By Ratha, MyBirthSign

What Wood means

Think of a young tree in spring: roots pushing down, branches reaching up, new leaves opening every week. That picture of steady, upward growth is the heart of Wood. It is the element of beginnings, of plans being sketched and seeds being planted, and of the patient strength that lets a bamboo shoot break through hard ground.

In Chinese the five elements are called wuxing, and Wood is mu. As Wikipedia's article on wuxing notes, the word was first translated into English as the Five Elements, but the idea is less about physical substances than about processes and change. That is why some scholars prefer translations such as five phases or five agents. Wood is best understood as a stage in a cycle, a kind of energy, rather than only a material.

Wood at a glance

Traditional Chinese cosmology links each element with a season, a direction, a colour and more. The New World Encyclopedia lists these correspondences for Wood:

  • Season: spring
  • Direction: east
  • Colour: green (qing, a colour between green and blue)
  • Planet: Jupiter
  • Heavenly creature: the Azure Dragon
  • Climate: windy
  • Heavenly Stems: Jia (yang) and Yi (yin)

Wood years

Every Chinese year is named by pairing one of ten Heavenly Stems with one of twelve Earthly Branches, the system the Hong Kong Observatory describes in its guide to the stems and branches. The branch gives the year's animal; the stem gives its element. Two stems belong to Wood: Jia, the yang form, and Yi, the yin form, as the table of stems in Wikipedia's article on the sexagenary cycle shows.

Because the ten stems repeat every ten years, each element covers two years in a row, once a decade. Wikipedia notes that 1984 was a Jia-zi year, the start of the present sixty-year cycle; counting from there gives a simple rule: Wood years end in 4 or 5. These are the Wood years from 1944 to 2035:

  • 1985 Wood Ox (Yi, yin)

2024 and 2025 were Wood years (the Wood Dragon and the Wood Snake), and the next Wood years are 2034 and 2035 (the Wood Tiger and the Wood Rabbit).

One important detail: the Chinese year starts at Chinese New Year, in late January or February, not on 1 January. Someone born in January 2024, before that year's Chinese New Year, still belongs to the previous year and its element. Our zodiac sign checker uses the exact new-year date for every year.

Yang and yin Wood

Each element appears twice in the cycle, once in a yang form and once in a yin form. Jia years are yang Wood, and they always fall on yang animals (Rat, Tiger, Dragon, Horse, Monkey and Dog). Yi years are yin Wood, and they always fall on yin animals (Ox, Rabbit, Snake, Goat, Rooster and Pig). In the list above, the years ending in 4 are yang and the years ending in 5 are yin.

Yang is traditionally the more outward, active side and yin the more inward, receptive side. Neither is better than the other: both are needed, and every element moves between them.

How Wood works with the other elements

The five elements are linked by two traditional cycles, which the New World Encyclopedia and Wikipedia both describe. In the generating cycle, each element feeds the next: Wood feeds Fire, Fire creates Earth, Earth bears Metal, Metal carries Water and Water nourishes Wood. In the overcoming cycle, each element keeps another in check: Wood parts Earth, Earth absorbs Water, Water quenches Fire, Fire melts Metal and Metal chops Wood. For Wood, that means:

  • Water supports Wood: Water nourishes Wood, just as rain makes plants grow.
  • Wood supports Fire: Wood feeds Fire, as dry branches keep a flame alive.
  • Metal restrains Wood: Metal chops Wood, as an axe fells a tree.
  • Wood restrains Earth: Wood parts Earth, as roots break up and hold the soil.

In compatibility readings, elements that follow each other in the generating cycle are traditionally said to support each other, and those linked by the overcoming cycle are said to need more balance. The Wood personality

In our readings on this site, the Wood element describes a style of energy that colours a person's zodiac sign. We describe it like this: Wood is the element of spring: expansion, new branches, new plans. People born in a Wood year are usually the ones pushing a group forward, pitching the next idea before the last one has even finished. They tend to be warm collaborators who genuinely want everyone around them to grow too, not just themselves. The flip side of that generosity is a tendency to overcommit: saying yes to one too many projects, or struggling to set boundaries with people who take advantage of their good nature.

Remember that the element is only one part of a traditional reading, alongside the animal, yin or yang, and in fuller readings the month, day and hour of birth. Two people with the same element can be very different, and the zodiac is a cultural tradition for reflection and fun, not a scientific test of character.

Living with Wood energy

Whatever your own element, traditional advice uses Wood as a reminder of certain qualities:

  • Start something: spring is Wood's season, so traditional advice links Wood with new projects, study and fresh starts.
  • Choose growth over perfection: a young tree grows a little every day rather than all at once.
  • Watch for overcommitting: Wood energy says yes easily, so leave room to rest.

Frequently asked questions

Which years are Wood years in the Chinese zodiac?

Years ending in 4 or 5, for example 2014, 2015, 2034 and 2035. The year starts at Chinese New Year, so January and early February births may belong to the previous year.

What season, direction and colour go with Wood?

Traditionally Wood goes with spring, east and green.

Which element supports Wood?

Water, in the generating cycle: Water nourishes Wood, just as rain makes plants grow. Wood in turn supports Fire.

Is my element the same as my zodiac animal?

No. Your animal comes from the year's Earthly Branch and your element from its Heavenly Stem. Each animal can have any of the five elements, so the same animal and element combination returns only every sixty years.
