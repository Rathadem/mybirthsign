Chinese Zodiac Guide

How to Calculate Your Chinese Zodiac Sign

✦ The right way — with the Lunar New Year date ✦

Three simple steps to find your correct Chinese zodiac sign, even if you were born in January or February.

Updated October 11, 2026 · MyBirthSign

Many quick zodiac charts simply match your birth year to an animal. That works for most birthdays, but not all: the Chinese zodiac year does not start on 1 January. It starts at Lunar New Year, which falls on a different day each year, between 21 January and 20 February. If you were born in that window, the calendar year and your zodiac year may not be the same.

Three Steps

  1. 1

    Find your birth date

    Use your full date of birth: day, month and year.

  2. 2

    Check that year’s Lunar New Year

    Zodiac years start at Lunar New Year, not 1 January. Born before it? You belong to the previous year’s sign.

  3. 3

    Match the year to an animal

    Find your zodiac year in the list below. The animals repeat every 12 years.

Tips for Accurate Results

  • Use the Lunar New Year date for your own birth year — it changes every year.
  • Born before Lunar New Year? Use the previous year’s animal and element.
  • The element changes on the same day as the animal, so both move together.
  • You do not need your birth time — the date is enough for the animal sign.
  • Some Chinese astrologers (BaZi) start the year at Lichun, the start of spring, around 4 February. This site uses Lunar New Year, the most common way.
  • The Khmer animal year changes at Khmer New Year in mid-April instead — see the Khmer lunar calendar.

Why Many Charts Get It Wrong

A simple year chart labels every day from 1 January to 31 December with one animal. For someone born on 1 February 1989 it says Snake, but Lunar New Year 1989 was on 6 February, so the correct sign is the Dragon of the 1988 zodiac year. The element shifts too: Earth Dragon, not Earth Snake. The MyBirthSign Checker looks up the real Lunar New Year date for every year from 1900 to 2060, so this is handled for you.
