How the Day's Animal Is Found: The Ancient Day Cycle Explained

Every daily fortune on this site begins with a line like “Today is a Monkey day”. That animal is not chosen by us: it comes from a count of days that has run without a break for well over two thousand years.

Published October 25, 2026 · MyBirthSign

Day cycle60 days
Animal cycle12 days
Example25/10/2026 is a Monkey day

Every day has a name

In the traditional Chinese calendar, days are not only numbered within a month. Each day also carries a name from a cycle of sixty, made by pairing ten Heavenly Stems with twelve Earthly Branches. The Hong Kong Observatory's guide explains that this Gan-Zhi cycle starts with Jia-Zi and is used to record years, months, days and hours, and that Gan-Zhi days have been used for two to three thousand years.

According to Wikipedia's article on the sexagenary cycle, the day count goes back to records of divination on oracle bones and has continued without a break since at least the Spring and Autumn period. In other words, every day of the past two and a half thousand years has its place in one long, unbroken count.

From the day's name to the day's animal

The animal comes from the branch, the second half of the day's name. Each of the twelve Earthly Branches belongs to one zodiac animal, in the usual order: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog and Pig. A day whose name ends in Shen, for example, is a Monkey day.

Because the branch moves on by one every day, the animals come round in order, one per day, and the same animal returns every twelve days. The full stem-and-branch name returns every sixty days, but for the animal only the twelve-day rhythm matters.

A worked example

Wikipedia's article on the sexagenary cycle works through one famous date: 1 October 1949 was a Jia-zi day, the first name in the cycle, so it was a Rat day. From that day you can find the animal of any other date by counting.

Take 25 October 2026. It is 28,148 days after 1 October 1949. Dividing by 12 leaves a remainder of 8. Counting 8 places on from the Rat (Rat is 0, Ox 1, Tiger 2, Rabbit 3, Dragon 4, Snake 5, Horse 6, Goat 7, Monkey 8) gives the Monkey, and our daily fortune for 25/10/2026 indeed opens with a Monkey day. Twelve days later, on 6 November 2026, the Monkey returns.

An independent example agrees. A published guide to almanac data gives the day pillar Wu-shen for 1 October 2026; its branch Shen is the Monkey, and the same count from 1949 also gives a Monkey day for that date.

The simple rule, step by step

You can find the animal of any date yourself:

  • Count the days from 1 October 1949 to your date (a date calculator or spreadsheet can do this).
  • Divide by 12 and keep the remainder, from 0 to 11.
  • Read the animal: 0 Rat, 1 Ox, 2 Tiger, 3 Rabbit, 4 Dragon, 5 Snake, 6 Horse, 7 Goat, 8 Monkey, 9 Rooster, 10 Dog, 11 Pig.

What the day's animal decides

Once the day's animal is known, the rest of the daily fortune follows from traditional relationships. The sign that matches the day's animal has a Great Day; the two signs in its triangle have a Good Day; the sign directly opposite it, the clash, is told to take it easy; and the others have an Ordinary Day. Our guide to zodiac clashes explains the triangles and the six opposite pairs, and the 12 zodiac hours explains how each sign's lucky time is chosen.

The day animal is not your zodiac sign

Your own zodiac sign comes from your birth year, which changes at Chinese New Year. The day's animal is different: it changes every day for everyone. Someone born in the Year of the Rabbit, for example, will meet a Rabbit day every twelve days, their Great Day, and a Rooster day, their clash, in between. Frequently asked questions

Why does every day have a zodiac animal?

Traditional Chinese timekeeping names every day in a continuous 60-day cycle of stems and branches. The branch of the day's name belongs to one of the twelve zodiac animals.

How often does the same day animal come back?

Every twelve days. The animals follow one another in the usual zodiac order, one per day.

How can I work out the animal of a date?

Count the days from 1 October 1949, a Rat day, divide by 12 and read the remainder: 0 Rat, 1 Ox, 2 Tiger and so on up to 11 Pig.

Is the day animal the same as my zodiac sign?

No. Your sign comes from your birth year; the day animal changes every day for everyone.
